**Title:** KOOL-AI-D(): The Machines Joined the Cult

**Short description:** A comic tour of machines, culture and creative credit asks who holds the cut, who can stop the process and what an open community should make possible.

## Abstract

The follow-up to “Linux is a cult, but a good cult” arrives with a new seating chart: the machines have joined, and the humans keep a hand on the brake. A deliberately mythic bus, a role-appropriate thermostat and a literary revision pipeline open practical questions about automation. When does capability fit the job? What does a revision or a generated rendering owe to its sources? Who holds the cut, and who can stop the process? The talk moves through open-source culture, curation and creative disagreement, then brings those questions back to the audience. Its central invitation is to raise the floor without turning personal taste into an admission test. The jokes are theatrical; the contributions, limits and available exits should be clear.

## Attendees will learn to

- Separate an entertaining automation story from evidence of capability and authority.
- Use role-fit, visible limits and an available return path when delegating work.
- Discuss creative credit and participation while leaving room for production-system criticism.

**Audience:** Open-source participants, developers, creators and AI-curious attendees; examples are explained without requiring a machine-learning background.
