## Proposal hook

The machines joined the Linux cult. The humans stayed sober.

## Long abstract (ready to paste)

A longtime Linux community joke returns with new hardware, new characters and an unexpectedly serious question: what happens when the tool that drank the Kool-Aid is also given the wheel?

The bus is an Osborne 1; the passengers are people, software and stories from open-source history. Through this deliberately comic setup, we test claims that capable systems should be trusted with consequential tasks. Role-fit matters more than a big benchmark. Real authority has to be bounded, watched and reversible. Provenance matters: an impressive remix should not hide the contributions and sources behind it.

The “Skynet tax” asks four grounded questions: What autonomy exists? What important resources can it affect? How could it fail? And who granted the authority? The talk ends with an exit test for open-source communities: enjoy what you like, share it without coercion, and give people credit. Raise the floor; don't move the gate.

## Three takeaways

- Linux culture is a useful lens on changing human/tool roles.
- Check the authority, resource, failure and permission path of agentic tools.
- Keep credit and a human exit route visible.

## Speaker bio

He brings a hands-on, playful perspective to community technology and creative practice.
