## Abstract (~250 words)
Ten years ago I gave my most-requested talk: "Linux is a cult, but a good cult." This is the follow-up, with news: the machines joined the cult. The humans stayed sober.

The bus is an Osborne 1 — the first computer you could take on the bus. We went further: it IS the bus. The bot drives because it volunteered — it drank the Kool-Aid. The humans ride in back with Tang and one hand on the brake. That's not a policy. That's a seating chart.

From there: role-fit beats raw spec (the dumbest device in the house isn't dumb if it fits its role); Tang has a documented return path; the counterculture pipeline (Kerouac's taped-together scroll as the longest CI pipeline in literature, Capote's "that's not writing, that's typing" template, and a maintainer who spoke while the patch was still in beta); and the authorship thesis — forty iterations are curation, every model has a hidden credit list, and the soul isn't mystical. It's provenance.

Then the Skynet tax: four questions every real AI incident report answers and no movie does — autonomy, consequential resources, failure path, and who granted the authority. The steel man has no glowing red eyes; it has API permissions.

And the exit test: enjoy what you like, stop pushing it on others, if they like it they can ask, give them credit. A cult is anything different than you. Raise the floor. Don't move the gate.

## Key takeaways
- Role-fit beats raw capability — in driving, in tools, in everything.
- Authority you can count, watch, and stop: the seating chart is the safety architecture.
- The soul isn't mystical — it's provenance; every model has a hidden credit list.
- The Skynet tax: four questions that separate real incidents from movie math.
- Raise the floor, don't move the gate — the exit test for everything in this talk.
